Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent urged European partners to help stabilise supplies, which have been disrupted by turmoil in the Persian Gulf and Ukrainian attacks on Russian refineries.

Over the past month, Brent's price has risen <mark style="background-color:#ffd591">**+7.52%**</mark>, and is up <mark style="background-color:#ffd591">**+59.15%**</mark> compared to the same time last year. - 🟩 **Refined-product tightness compounding:** China's major refiners have suspended most refined fuel exports for October.

---

### ⭐ OPEC+ SUNDAY (Oct 4) — the weekend's key energy event

- 🟨 🟩 Countries participating in the monthly production adjustments of OPEC+ are set to meet on <mark style="background-color:#ffd591">**Sunday, October 4, 2026**</mark>. The meeting is expected to set production targets for the following month, amid an incomplete recovery of physical supply.
- 🟨 🟩 **Consensus = rollover:** A rollover of October targets at the September 2026 level is anticipated, though the official decision has yet to be confirmed by the countries involved.
- 🟥 🟩 **Why OPEC is a sideshow now:** The expected decision comes at an unusual time. With the Strait of Hormuz disrupting exports, the amount of crude reaching global markets is being shaped more heavily by <mark style="background-color:#ffd591">**wartime shipping constraints**</mark> than by adjustments to OPEC+ production targets. That has limited the group's traditional ability to manage supply and influence prices.

---

> 🟥 **READ:** Oil is now a *two-sided tug-of-war*: **war-premium** (Hormuz, three carriers, Saudi-Houthi offensive, weekly tanker strikes) vs. **policy-suppression** (G7's 100M-barrel release, diesel front-loaded, OPEC+ rollover, flows back to ~80%). Friday's print — Brent flat near $102, WTI down ~2% — shows the suppression side winning *at the margin*, but the floor is structurally high: Brent is **+59% y/y**. The real stress is in <mark style="background-color:#ff8f8f">**distillates/diesel**</mark>, which is why the G7 front-loaded diesel. Base case: Brent holds the high-$90s to low-$100s; the asymmetry is to the upside on any post-midterm kinetic signal. Prefer **integrated majors (<mark style="background-color:#ffd591">XOM 164.01</mark>) over refiners** — the diesel release compresses crack spreads that have been fattening <mark style="background-color:#ff8f8f">VLO (406.30)/MPC (422.33)/PSX (264.58)</mark>.

## 5. 🏦 FEDERAL RESERVE — OCTOBER HIKE "JUST DIED"; DECEMBER STILL THE BASE CASE

- 🟨 🟩 **October collapse:** CME's FedWatch tool shows only a <mark style="background-color:#6ee7a8">**17% chance**</mark> that the Fed increases rates by a quarter percentage point. One week ago, odds were close to **36%**. On Kalshi, chances for a hike in October stood at just **18%**, down from almost **70%** a week ago.
- 🟨 🟩 **But December holds:** While traders now think a hike in October is unlikely, they're still forecasting that the central bank will raise rates in December. On FedWatch, odds are above <mark style="background-color:#ff8f8f">**75%**</mark> for a hike in December, while on Kalshi there are **65%** odds that the Fed raises rates.
- 🟩 **The inflation offset to the soft jobs:** Odds for a hike in October also fell after the PCE index on Wednesday showed cooler-than-expected prices. Core prices, excluding food and energy, rose **3%** in August, lighter than consensus estimates for a rise of **3.3%**.
- 🟩 **The September hike that set the stage:** The Fed delivered its first hike in more than three years, lifting rates **25 basis points** in a unanimous **12-0 vote**. 16 of 19 FOMC members expect at least one more rate hike this year. The Fed's target range is now **3.75% to 4%**.
- 🔴 🟩 **The AI-inflation worry (Governor Cook):** Fed Governor Lisa Cook said in a Sept. 28 speech that in the short term, AI appears to be <mark style="background-color:#ff8f8f">**adding inflationary pressures**</mark> to the economy, postponing inflation's return to the 2% target. *"In coming months I expect to see continued pressure on inflation from the AI buildout and from the pass-through of higher oil prices and supply chain disruptions associated with the conflict in the Middle East."*
- 🟩 **Street consensus:** Lindsay Rosner of Goldman Sachs Asset Management agrees a rate increase is unlikely this month, but that it's likely the Fed's hiking cycle isn't over. *"One follow-up hike in December remains our base case; however, continued pressure by markets and moves higher in energy prices could force the Fed's hand this month as well."*
- 🟩 **The next decision:** The Federal Reserve is set to announce its next decision on interest rates at the conclusion of a two-day policy meeting on <mark style="background-color:#ffd591">**Oct. 28**</mark>.

---

> 🟥 **READ:** This is a *hiking* cycle in a cooling labor market — the mirror image of a normal late-cycle setup — because the inflation impulse is coming from **fiscal + geopolitical + AI-capex sources the Fed can't easily tame.** October is effectively off the table (~17%), but December is still the base case (~75% FedWatch), and the "triggers" to kill December are specific: the unemployment rate breaking to **4.4%+** or oil rolling over hard. Governor Cook naming the AI buildout *and* the Iran war as inflation drivers is the honest framing. <mark style="background-color:#ffd591">**Position for a Fed that pauses in October but keeps the hammer cocked for December.**</mark>
