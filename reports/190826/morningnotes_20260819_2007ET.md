## 4. 📉 TREASURY YIELDS — GLOBAL BOND ROUT, THEN A TREASURY INTERVENTION SLAMS THE BRAKES

- 🔴 🟩 [green]**Tuesday's record:**[/green] The U.S. 30Y Treasury yield briefly eclipsed 5.337%, its highest point in nearly two decades. The U.S. 10-year yield pushed toward 4.75%, while the 30-year climbed above 5.3%, its highest since 2007.
- ⭐ 🟩 [green]**The intervention — the day's biggest policy action:**[/green] The Treasury Department said Wednesday it will more than double the size of its government debt repurchases, sending yields sharply lower; the announcement targets the 10- to 20-year and 20- to 30-year portion of the market, which has seen a buyers' strike since late June. The government will "at least double" the maximum size of its buyback operations, from $2 billion to "at least" $4 billion — yields cratered following the announcement.
- 🟩 [green]**Where yields sit now:**[/green] The 10-year note yield was almost 1 basis point lower at 4.696%; the 2-year yield fell more than 1 bp to 4.1602%; the 30-year bond yield was last flat at 5.283%, after notching a new 19-year high on Tuesday at over 5.33%.
- ⚠️ 🟩 [green]**The structural drivers of the rout:**[/green] Strategists ascribe the run to intensified concerns over a budget deficit set to eclipse its 2025 level; inflation in a holding pattern above the Fed's 2% target; and a rash of corporate debt issuance competing with Treasurys — plus a rising term premium.
- ⚠️ 🟩 [green]**The debt-load overhang:**[/green] Treasury yields are climbing at a particularly bad time as higher rates worsen the impact of the nearly $40 trillion government debt load.

> 🟥 [red]**READ:**[/red] The Treasury buyback is the tell — this is a [red]**quasi-official recognition that the long end had become disorderly.**[/red] Doubling buybacks into the 10-30y "buyers' strike" zone is a targeted attempt to cap term premium without formally easing policy, and it worked for a day (yields cratered). But it treats the symptom, not the disease: the disease is oil-driven inflation risk + a $40T debt load + a global bond exodus (JGBs at 30-yr highs, bunds at 15-yr highs). The buyback buys time; it doesn't fix the deficit or bring oil down. [red]**Bias: the long end stays structurally pressured**[/red] — fade rallies in duration unless oil rolls over meaningfully. The front end (2Y ~4.16%) is the calmer, Fed-anchored part of the curve; a bull-steepener is the cleaner risk/reward than outright long duration here.
